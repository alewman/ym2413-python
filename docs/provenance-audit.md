# Provenance audit of the behaviour specification

Done 2026-11-13, before any chip code was written, under rules 9 to 11 of
[restart.md](restart.md). The research session reported having seen, before it
recognised them, a passage quoting a GPL model, three small tables copied from a
GPL emulator, and unlicensed C fragments. This audit does not take its word that
[spec.md](spec.md) is free of them, and does not re-read those passages to check.
It goes the other way: for every statement, find the support in the cited
source's **prose**, or in a measurement the source reports, and write down the
chain.

## How the sources were read

- **S1, Yamaha's application manual.** The OCR text of every page for the prose;
  printed pages 9, 10, 11, 12, 13 and 16 as images for Tables III-1, III-2, III-4
  to III-7, III-9 and Figure III-1. Nothing else of S1 was needed.
- **S4, the capture project's write-ups.** Through a filter written for this audit
  (not committed: it lives in the git-ignored `reference/scratch/`). It hides every
  line that looks like program text (a trailing semicolon, braces, C keywords,
  shifts, subscripts, bit operators), everything inside a code fence, the whole of
  `rhythm.md` from the heading "## Nuke.YKT" to the heading "## [2020/01/02]
  Update", and, for the three write-ups that print tables copied from an emulator
  (`20150320.txt`, `20151128/am.txt`, `20151201.txt`), every line that is mostly
  numbers. What was left was read in full for 18 of the 21 files.
  `20150327/attack-rates.txt` and `20170126/formula-f1-4.txt` were not read
  (the spec cites neither; `attack.txt` supersedes the first), and
  `20150409/predicted-sine.txt` is a table of a model's output and was not opened.
- **S6, the NESdev wiki page.** Whole file.
- **S12, emu2413 (MIT).** Its `LICENSE`, lines 1 to 224 (the instrument tables and
  constant tables with their comments), and the functions that make the percussion
  sounds, key the percussion slots and look up the exponential table (about 150
  lines in all). Permissive source may be read (rule 4); nothing was ported. It is
  used below only as rule 10 allows: as a named, emulator-tier default where
  nothing better exists, and as corroboration.
- **S2, S7, S8, S9, S11, S13, S14** were not re-read. Statements that rest on
  them alone are marked "not re-checked" below; none of them carries a number the
  core computes with, except S11's two formulas, which S4's prose restates.
- A scratch file left by the research session, `reference/scratch/attack_f.txt`,
  was not opened: its contents are unknown and may be program text.

## What the filter let through (slips, reported in full)

1. **Formula lines inside S4's running text.** Lines such as the phase step
   ("step-size = ..."), "eg_shift = 13 - (rate / 4)" and "Set m = 16 - (rate / 4)"
   are written as part of a sentence or a bullet list, not in a block, and three
   were displayed before the filter was tightened (the first of them) or because
   they contain no operator the filter looks for. Each is the author's statement of
   a result he then checks against a printed table of measurements; the spec
   statements they bear on (7.3, 8.5, 8.12) are supported by those tables and by
   prose, as listed below.
2. **Two rows of an emulator's key-scale table** were displayed from `ksl.txt`
   (sixteen numbers, the 6 dB per octave setting in envelope steps), under the
   sentence "The emulators contain the exact same tables, generated by a piece of
   code similar to this". The same sixteen numbers are Yamaha's Table III-5,
   bottom row, multiplied by 16/3; the core computes them from Yamaha's row
   (spec 9.2) and the emulator's rows are not used.
3. **The step patterns of 8.5 and the tremolo description** appear in S4's prose
   both as "what the emulators do" and as what S4 then measured. They were read in
   the second form (`adsr-transitions.txt`, `am.txt`); see the entries for 8.5 and
   10.2.
4. **S4's list of sixteen boolean forms for the cymbal** is a numbered list in
   running text, not a code block, and was read. The meaning of its names ("c85",
   "m77") is given only in a code block, which was not read; see 11.9.
5. **More of emu2413 was read than the tables**: the percussion functions sit next
   to the carrier and modulator functions and the exponential look-up, and those
   were on screen. They are MIT and may be read. What they showed is recorded as
   emulator-tier corroboration under 6.5, 6.11 and 6.12, and the core's arithmetic
   is written from the spec's statements, not from those lines.

## Every statement and its chain

Sources, once:

- **S1**: Yamaha, "FM Operator Type-LL (OPLL) Application Manual". The
  manufacturer's own statement. Scanned by Ricardo Bittencourt, made into a PDF by
  HansO in 2001; read from the SMS Power copy. Nothing upstream.
- **S4**: Wouter Vermaelen (openMSX), write-ups in the repository
  `andete/ym2413` (Joost Yervante Damad's) at commit `4893d1c`, 2015 to 2020. No
  licence. Method: one real YM2413 on a board with an ARM micro-controller that
  drives the chip's pins and clock and samples MO and RO with a 12-bit ADC, 16
  times oversampled, the chip clocked slowly; ADC values mapped back to the chip's
  9-bit codes through a calibrated histogram. Upstream is noted per statement
  where S4 names one. The write-ups also print C fragments and quote two
  emulators; those were not used.
- **S6**: NESdev wiki, "VRC7 audio" (wiki authors; the snapshot pinned in
  `fetch_references.py`). Upstream: two forum posts it cites (p=224897,

  ROMs of a YM3812 and a YMF262 read bit by bit under a microscope. Known here
  through S4's prose restatement of its two formulas.

## New unknowns

- **U22.** The hi-hat's four phases and which pairs with which value of the noise
  bit and of the cymbal's bit. Default: emu2413's, read with the snare's measured
  noise polarity.
- **U23.** For effective rates 48 and up: which bits of the shared counter choose
  the faster of two attack formulas, and where the 16-sample decay cycles of 8.7
  sit against the counter. Default: bits 2 and 3, as the slow-rate rule continued.
- **U24.** How a negative modulator output rounds when it is doubled into the
  carrier's phase and when two are added and shifted for feedback. Default: one's
  complement first, then an arithmetic shift.

## What this audit does not show

It shows that each statement has support in prose, a printed measurement, or a
named permissive emulator. It does not show that the research session's memory of
what it had seen left no trace in the wording of the spec; no reading of the spec
could. Where the spec's numbers go beyond what the prose supports, they have been
struck above.
